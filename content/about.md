# About Saiprapul Thotapally

I'm driven by curiosity more than anything else. I started out as a mechanical engineer, and I never lost the need to know how a thing works one layer down. These days that curiosity points at AI, and at one moment in particular: when a system stops answering and starts acting for someone. What makes a person comfortable handing over that next step is the question I care about most.

I learn by building. Hand me ten articles and I'll probably understand the idea, but for me the practical is where the rubber meets the road. It's what keeps me up late, talking with Claude and other agents, following a question down the rabbit hole until I've actually found out what's at the bottom of it.

## How I think

- **From first principles** — I don't fully trust a system I can't explain from the layer beneath it, which is why I've gone as far down as how a processor turns electricity into a running program.
- **In probabilities** — a confident answer is only worth something if the confidence means something. I'd rather be wrong in a way I can measure than right in a way I can't explain.
- **Brainstorm, then build** — I think out loud first, with people and with agents, until the shape of an idea shows up. Then I build the smallest version that can prove me wrong.

## Questions I keep coming back to

- When has a system earned the right to act? Autonomy isn't a setting, it's earned per action.
- What happens when it's wrong? Every AI product is partly a recovery product.
- What does an intent-based interface look like? None of the old shapes (terminal, wizard, chat) fit software that generates its own interface — following A2UI closely.
- What changes when voice is the primary interface? I think it's coming, and it raises the same problems every ambient interface will have to solve.

## Where my free time goes

- Experimenting to keep up: Claude Code, OpenClaw, Goose, running the same problem through different frontier models. What I learn goes into a wiki structured so an agent can read it as well as I can.
- Learning how things work from the silicon up — a long-running path through semiconductors and chip design that produced the processor and transformer explainers.
- A few unfinished takes: how prediction markets price uncertainty, and whether old mathematical philosophy has anything to teach modern markets.
- Agentic commerce, as a curiosity — reputation, not identity, is what I think will decide whether people let an agent spend their money.
