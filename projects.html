AI-First Voice Ordering (Global Payments)

Roadmap & strategy · POC

Voice-first order capture on a Genius handheld—a server takes a table's order conversationally instead of tapping through screens. I own the thesis: where voice wins versus where the category is commoditizing, build-vs-buy-vs-partner per lane, and a beachhead sequence that proves the agent core before spend commits to the next channel. The hard part isn't transcription—it's attributing who said what, and applying a mid-order change to the right person's order.

Decisions: segment selection · channel sequencing · build-vs-buy-vs-partner · pricing · dialog confidence policy

Bar: ≤500ms p50 / ≤800ms p95 voice-to-screen · ≥90% speaker attribution under overlap · ≤1.5× word-error ratio across accent subgroups · five-phase ramp

Three working prototypes to settle architecture before engineering committed; speech-to-speech POC demoed live to customers

Agentic Commerce & Agent Reputation

Independent research

Agents are starting to transact for people, and the infrastructure has converged on one half of the problem. The protocols answer who is this agent; almost nothing answers is this agent any good—whether it honors terms, whether its last counterparty would deal with it again. Reputation, not identity, is what gates letting an agent spend someone's money, and it's the layer with real network effects once someone builds it credibly.

Same question as the day job, one altitude up: a POS assistant earns the right to act for one operator; a commerce agent has to earn it across a network.

Context: third-party analysts put agent-orchestrated US retail near $1T by 2030, roughly a fifth of commerce happening agent-to-agent—against about 1% of shoppers buying through agents today
